## Protection of Trees By-law No. 9958

Consolidated to 9 December 2025; amended to include By-law No. 14546 effective
1 January 2026.

### Definition — "protection barrier" (s. 1.2)

A barrier erected to protect a tree and its roots that:

- **(a)** is at least **1.2 metres high** measured from the ground;
- **(b)** meets the distance requirements, **measured 1.4 m above the existing
  grade** of the ground adjoining the base of the tree, set out in Schedule A;
- **(c)** consists of snow fencing fastened securely to metal or wood stakes spaced
  no more than one metre apart, or other fencing acceptable to the Chief Building
  Official or City Engineer, or as otherwise approved by the Director of Planning;
- **(d)** is acceptable to the Chief Building Official (on site or adjacent
  property) or the City Engineer (on a street).

Related: "drip line" is defined as a circle on the ground directly under the tips
of the outermost branches of the canopy. It is a **separate** thing from the
Schedule A distance, and the two should not be conflated in a tool.

|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 | 1.2 | 0.060 |
| 25 | 1.5 | 0.060 |
| 30 | 1.8 | 0.060 |
| 35 | 2.1 | 0.060 |
| 40 | 2.4 | 0.060 |
| 45 | 2.7 | 0.060 |
| 50 | 3.0 | 0.060 |
| 55 | 3.3 | 0.060 |
| 60 | 3.6 | 0.060 |
| 75 | 4.5 | 0.060 |
| **90** | **5.0** | **0.056** |
| 100 | 6.0 | 0.060 |

**Three gaps a tool would have to take a position on.**

1. **The 90 cm row breaks the pattern.** Every other row is exactly 0.06 m per cm.
   At 90 cm that gives 5.4 m; the schedule says 5.0 m. At 100 cm it returns to
   0.06. Deliberate exception or consolidated-forward error — unresolved.
2. **Intermediate diameters are undefined.** A 43 cm tree has no row. Round up to
   45, interpolate between 40 and 45, or apply 0.06 directly? The schedule does not
   say, and the three answers differ by up to 0.3 m.
3. **Above 100 cm is undefined.** Vancouver has many trees over 100 cm.

Worth noting against Schedule A: **Schedule B solves the same problem properly.**
It uses explicit closed bands and an open top row. Two schedules in one by-law
drafted to different standards is itself a useful thing for a tool to be able to
show.

| 2551 – 2900 | 20 |
| 2901 – 3250 | 25 |
| over 3250 | 30, plus one more per 50 m² above 3250 m² |

**Boundary defects, as printed.** 365 m² appears in both the first and second band.
450–451 m² falls between bands, as does 1000–1001 and every later boundary. A tool
has to resolve these and should say how.

A point of contact with the preferred source: a 500 m² R1-1 lot requires 4 trees
here, while R1-1 s. 3.2.2.8 permits 75% of it to be impermeable. Whether those two
by-laws are satisfiable together on a small lot is a real design question and a
possible later direction.

## Engineering Design Manual 2026 — Table 9-2

s. 9.3.3.2 Spacing and Soil Volume Requirements, p. 367 of 420.
**Engineering guidance, not a by-law.**

| Tree size category | Average spacing | Soil volume, solitary tree | Soil volume, shared / row |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Large | 9.0–11 m | 30 m³ | 20 m³ |
| Medium | 8.0–10 m | 20 m³ | 15 m³ |
| Small | 7.0–10 m | 10 m³ | 5.0 m³ |
| Columnar | 7.0–10 m | 20 m³ | 15 m³ |

Qualifications carried in the surrounding text:

- Native soil is preferred. Where it cannot be obtained, volume may be met with
  **structural soil, calculated at 50% of its volume**, or with soil cells where
  approved by the City Engineer. A 30 m³ requirement met in structural soil
  therefore needs a 60 m³ trench.
- Structural soil and soil cells are permitted **only for new trees**; soil cells
  only during new road construction where utilities are not impacted.
- "Tree size category" is **not defined in the table** and has to be resolved
  against a species list elsewhere.

The manual also confirms the division of responsibility relied on above: private
property trees over 20 cm diameter are administered under Protection of Trees
By-law No. 9958 (s. 9.3.2.3).
