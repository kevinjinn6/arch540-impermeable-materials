# Outside review: R1-1 Hard Surface Map (pipeline, page and evidence)

Reviewer lens: computational design / geospatial engineering. Reviewed cold on 2026-10-02 from the public checkout (HEAD 54ada95, byte-identical to the live page and the live data files) and the cached City data. Nothing was rendered in a browser; all numbers below were recomputed with python3 or the project's geopandas environment.

## Overall verdict

The pipeline is honest, well commented and, on the arithmetic it claims, correct. Every headline number reproduces exactly from `map/data/lots.geojson`: 62,248 in-scope sites (`sc = 0`), median site 403.02 m², 39.06 % of a 48 mm storm held at the 75 % setting, 717,310 m³ (286.9 pools) overflowing, soil full at 39.8 % hardscape. My independent clip of the 154,124 City footprints to the 63,031 sites matched the committed `r` values to within 0.005 m² on every site, footprint self-overlap is 0.01 % of roof area (no double counting), and the address-and-edge merge rule does what its docstring says (635 merged sites, 21 flagged non-adjoining). The water-balance code, the formula printed on the page and the README prose agree with each other line for line.

The problem is what the correct arithmetic is applied to. The page's most prominent number, **13,594 "potential roof-coverage exceedances"**, is almost entirely an artefact of measuring roof outlines instead of walls: shrinking the footprints by a 0.3 m eave drops the count to 2,593, and by 0.6 m to 249. The water card, meanwhile, is presented as a citywide summation across 62,248 lots but is in fact a one-line formula times total area; no lot's shape or roof enters it. Add unpinned dependencies, a data snapshot that cannot be regenerated to the same numbers, and a CONFIGURATION block whose values do not propagate to a dozen hard-coded strings, and the tool is less changeable and less evidential than its headers promise. None of this is hidden dishonesty; the caveats exist in comments. But a visitor to the live page sees a 22 px amber 13,594 before any caveat, and that is what this review is about.

## Findings

**1. MAJOR. The 13,594 / 21.8 % roof-coverage exceedance count is dominated by the eave artefact.**
Evidence: the City describes the 2015 dataset as "the generalized outermost exterior outline of main building components generated from the City of Vancouver's 2015 orthophotos" (portal metadata, fetched today). Section 2 (June 2026, p. 20) defines impermeable materials as "the projected area of the outside of the outermost walls of all buildings, including carports, entries, porches and verandahs"; s. 4.2.2 (R1-1 schedule p. 16) folds site coverage into that. `buildings.py` lines 47-51 admit the footprints "follow the ROOF EDGE, eaves included … typically 0.3-0.6 m". I recomputed with the project's geopandas (EPSG:26910, `make_valid`, STRtree pairs, vectorised intersection; 33 s):

| eave shrink | in-scope sites > 50 % | > 75 % | total roof area |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 m (as shipped) | 13,595 | 16 | 11.94 M m² |
| 0.3 m | 2,593 | 7 | −11.2 % |
| 0.45 m | 804 | 4 | −16.6 % |
| 0.6 m | 249 | 3 | −21.8 % |

9,725 of the 13,594 flagged sites sit in the 50-55 % band; a 10 × 12 m house loses 10.7 % of its area to a 0.3 m eave. Why it matters: the number is rendered at 22 px amber (`index.html` 692, styled by `.risk-count b` at 128), drives 13,594 amber lot outlines and every cluster circle, and the only caveat is 11 px grey text. A planner would read it as 1 in 5 houses over the limit; the defensible range is roughly 250-2,600 (0.4-4 %). Fix: add `EAVE_M = 0.45` to the `buildings.py` CONFIGURATION, negative-buffer footprints (`join_style="mitre"`) before `clip_buildings`, store both `r` and the eave-corrected value, and show the count as a range with the assumption named; or raise the flag threshold to 55-60 % and label it "check against survey".

**2. MAJOR. The water card is independent of the lot data; the summation is cosmetic.**
Evidence: `waterBalance` (`index.html` 582-590) is linear in `areaM2`, so `summarise` (592-599) over 62,248 lots equals `totalArea × min(P(1−h) + P·C·h, D(1−h)) / 1000`. I evaluated both: identical to the cubic metre at every slider value (e.g. h = 0.75: loop 717,310 m³, closed form 717,310 m³; 39.06 % both ways). The only data-dependent input is Σ A = 30.076 km². The popup therefore reports the same soak-in percentage for every lot, and the mapped roofs play no part in any water figure. README lines 60-71 ("For each site it estimates … and sums across sites") and the card footer "across 62,248 ordinary R1-1 lots" imply lot-level modelling that does not occur. Why it matters: the tool's two halves, roofs and water, never meet, and a reader cannot tell. Fix: either say so plainly ("every lot behaves the same: at 75 % hardscape 18.75 of 48 mm soak in") and present per-m² depths, or couple them: use `max(slider, roofShare)` as each site's hard share so that lots differ and the roof data matters. Also remove the O(n) loop from the slider handler or keep it only as a self-check.

**3. MAJOR. Not reproducible from a fresh clone; the data snapshot is unverifiable.**
Evidence: README line 124 installs five packages unpinned; there is no `requirements.txt`, `pyproject.toml` or lock file (checked). `.gitignore` line 19 ignores `map/data/` yet both files are tracked (`git check-ignore --no-index` confirms the contradiction; they were force-added in f18b85f as single-line 17 MB and 36 MB blobs). The parcel layer "is updated weekly" (portal metadata, modified 2026-09-28) and `cache/` is not committed, so re-running today will not give 62,248. Neither script records a retrieval date or record count in its output. The self-tests in both scripts test only the perm-min algebra; the merge, clip and web-export steps and all page JavaScript have no tests. `pages.yml` deploys whatever is in `map/` with no check that data and code agree. Why it matters: a maintainer who edits `buildings.py` and recommits will silently change every published number. Fix: `requirements.txt` pinned to the working environment (geopandas 1.0.1, shapely 2.0.7, pandas 2.3.3, numpy 2.0.2, pyproj 3.6.1, pyogrio 0.11.1); have `buildings.py` write `map/data/manifest.json` (dataset ids, portal `modified` stamps, record counts, md5, key counts such as `sc` tallies); remove `map/data/` from `.gitignore`; add a 20-line smoke test run by the workflow before deploy.

**4. MAJOR. The CONFIGURATION contract does not hold; a non-programmer cannot change the numbers safely.**
Evidence: `index.html` lines 27-28 and 218 promise "change these numbers, not the code below", but 50 %, 75 % and 48 mm are hard-coded in page text at lines 270, 272, 648-650, 654, 656, 693-694, 697 and in static HTML at 173 and 178. The roof colours are set in `render()` (559-561) and again for the swatches (280-281); the light-mode swatch `#3d3d3a` does not match the dark-mode roof `#6b6a65`. The hydrology constants are declared three times with different values: `stormwater.py` 109/115 (C = 0.9, D = 30), `buildings.py` 96/101 (C = 0.85, D ∈ {30,48,75,285}), `index.html` 222-224 (C = 0.85, D = 75). Nothing the Python computes for water (`perm_min_m2_d*`, `roof_blocks_d*`) is read by the page; it is dead weight in a 153 MB intermediate. Why it matters: changing `BYLAW_MAX_BUILDING_RATIO` to 0.6 yields a page that still says "50 %" in six places. Fix: template every by-law and storm string from the constants; move the colour trio into CONFIGURATION; delete or clearly mark the unused hydrology fields; put the three constants in one `config.json` read by both Python and the page.

**5. MINOR. Scope flags admit non-lots; the sparse flags are unused.**
Evidence: four "1 CASSIAR CONNECTOR" highway sites are in scope (`sc = 0`, e.g. SITE-R11-017054, 1,494 m², no roof); 249 in-scope sites have no address, 239 of them roofless (99,584 m²); 869 in-scope sites have no roof at all (443,014 m², 1.5 % of area). `write_web` (`buildings.py` 524-528) documents `f` (54 features), `cd` (3) and `m` (635); the page never reads `f` or `cd` (grep), and `m` only drives a label. The three `cd` lots (roof 94-100 %) are counted among the 16 "roofs alone exceed 75 %". Seventeen out-of-scope sites have roof share > 50 %; because the building shader carries no scope attribute, their footprints draw amber or purple on grey lots with no outline. Fix: treat "no address and no roof" as not-a-lot; have `cd` suppress the exceedance claim; drop `f`/`cd` from the web file or use them; pass scope to the building mesh.

**6. MINOR. Shader logic is correct but has visible edge cases.**
(a) Lot shader (`index.html` 331-335) colours by `v_y ≥ 1 − u_hard`, where `v_y` is the bounding-box height fraction (368-369), not an area fraction: on a 45°-rotated lot 25 % of height is 12.5 % of area, so the red band under-represents hardscape on Vancouver's angled grids. (b) Footprints appear at their true position, so a "revealed" roof can lie entirely in the blue permeable band; the legend's spatial reading breaks. (c) Building shader (455-468) uses `precision mediump float`; on mobile GPUs that is fp16 (ulp ≈ 0.0005 near 0.5), so the 1e-5 epsilon vanishes and the 220 buildings with `rs` in (0.5, 0.5005] plus 45 at exactly 0.5 may colour differently from their lot's CPU-computed amber outline. (d) The two discard clauses (461-463) reduce to `if (u_hard + eps < min(v_rs, u_maxImpermeable)) discard;`. (e) Buildings whose `rs` > 0.75 "appear" only at 75 %, contradicting "appears when the slider reaches its share"; say so. Fix: `highp`, or pass an integer colour class from JavaScript; state that the band is a proportion of lot height.

**7. MINOR. Phone performance is unmeasured and the page does the heavy work on the main thread.**
Evidence: GitHub Pages gzips, so the wire cost is 2.3 + 6.1 = 8.4 MB, not 53 MB; the README should say so. The real cost is `JSON.parse` of 53 MB of text, 104,579 `earcut` calls, 324,510 + 930,325 vertices (about 5 and 11 MB of VBOs), plus MapLibre's own worker copy of the lots source. The building layer issues roughly 2.2 M triangle indices and 1.9 M line indices in two draw calls every frame at zoom ≥ 14 with no culling (550-568). 4.3 MB of `buildings.geojson` is `id`, `a`, `h`, `src`, which the page never reads. I could not run a browser; a budget mid-range phone is at real risk of a multi-second freeze or a memory-killed tab. Fix: strip unused properties, consider 5 decimals, fetch footprints only when zoom ≥ 14, parse in a Worker, or move to vector tiles.

**8. MINOR. Error handling covers one failure mode.**
Evidence: the lots fetch has a visible fallback (744-749). A failed basemap style, a blocked CDN (43-47, no SRI hashes) or missing WebGL leaves "Loading about 62,000 lots…" forever: there is no `map.on("error")` and no check that `maplibregl` loaded. A failed footprint fetch is `console.warn` only (903-905) while the legend still promises roofs. A null `OES_vertex_array_object` throws at 398. Fix: an error handler that replaces the loading text; a visible "footprints unavailable" notice; SRI.

**9. MINOR. Accessibility.**
Evidence: the slider is labelled and the stops are real buttons (good). But the notes text `#85847e` on `#fcfcfb` is 3.65:1 at 11 px (AA needs 4.5:1); permeable blue vs impermeable red is 1.07:1 luminance, hue-only; material labels are 8 px (884); every per-lot result requires a mouse click on a canvas, with no keyboard route, address search or table alternative; the popup receives no focus. Fix: darken `--ink-3`; add an address search box that opens the same popup; add a pattern or hatch for one material.

**10. MINOR. Vintage and documentation drift.**
Evidence: footprints are 2015 (11 years; the City itself says "significant number of changes since collected year … best used for comparison purposes only"), so post-2015 laneway houses and rebuilds are absent while demolished houses persist; 2009 LiDAR heights are carried in the web file but unused. README "Repository map" (28-42) omits `buildings.py`, `map/data/`, `.github/`; "Not in this repository" (162-168) says GeoJSON outputs are not committed while 53 MB are; `buildings.py` line 33 says "~80 MB" but the two footprint downloads are 97 + 76 MB. The wrap-up requires one example with a screenshot; none is in the README. The repository has zero issues as of today, so there is no in-repo peer-review evidence yet. The brief's Rhino visualisation and reusable skill are not in this checkout.

## What works

The engineering that is there is careful. Parcel ids are minted after a stable sort (`stormwater.py` 898-901); downloads are validated before caching and written atomically (266-331); invalid geometries are repaired; areas are computed in UTM 10N before any export; the web files use short keys and 0.3 m simplification with areas taken from full geometry; coordinates are stored relative to a Mercator origin and the origin is folded back into the camera matrix in double precision (425-426), which is exactly how to avoid float32 jitter. The site definition is cited correctly from Section 2 p. 43, the merge tolerance logic (`adjoin`, 296-306) is sound and verified, the popup uses `textContent` so addresses cannot inject markup, dark mode is handled, and the self-tests, caveat blocks and plain-language comments are far above student norm. Most importantly, every number I could check, I could reproduce.

## The three changes that would matter most

1. **Correct for eaves before counting exceedances** (Finding 1): one configurable buffer in `buildings.py`, the count shown as a range, the assumption named next to the number.
2. **Make the water card either honest or coupled** (Finding 2): say it is one formula for every lot, or let each site's mapped roof set its floor so the roofs and the water finally talk to each other.
3. **Pin, manifest and test** (Findings 3-4): `requirements.txt`, a data manifest written by the pipeline, one smoke test in the Pages workflow, and every by-law string templated from CONFIGURATION so the "change the numbers, not the code" promise is true.
