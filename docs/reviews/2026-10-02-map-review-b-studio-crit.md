# Outside review: "Impermeable materials in Vancouver R1-1" (live map, repo as published 2026-10-02)

Reviewed cold from the live page (byte-identical to `map/index.html` at commit 54ada95), the public repository, the R1-1 District Schedule (June 2026, pp. 12 and 16), Section 2 Definitions (June 2026, pp. 20 and 31), the assignment brief (v3) and the A1 wrap-up sheet. Line numbers refer to `map/index.html` unless stated. Numbers were recomputed from `map/data/lots.geojson`.

## Verdict

The README states a sharp, genuinely teachable piece of design knowledge: under Section 2, "permeable pavers" are impermeable and wood decking is permeable only "with spaced boards" (README.md:10–15; Section 2 pp. 20, 31). The published tool does not contain that knowledge. The words paver, decking, spaced, gravel, Section 2 and definition do not occur anywhere in `index.html`. The by-law enters the page as two constants, `0.75` and `0.50` (231–234), and two section labels. What the tool actually computes is a Rain City 48 mm water balance (582–590) that the student's own source register says "is **not** a requirement" and "must be described that way" (sources/README.md:49–56). The slider is a single number applied uniformly to 62,248 lots (429), clamped at the by-law maximum (165), so the rule has no fail state. This is a competent, even impressive, stormwater visualization wearing a by-law caption. As a piece of interactive design knowledge about s. 3.2.2.8, it is a decoration on a dataset.

Measured against the W1 work, it is a step sideways. The high-context prototype (`week-01/prompt-b-high-context/r1-1-coverage-checker.jsx`) classified each surface by the Section 2 lists, carried a clause reference on every rule, separated what was read from what was computed, and the write-up proved the point with a 500 m² lot that passes under one reading and fails under the other (docs/prompt-comparison.md:64–83). That is Explain, Compute and Reuse as the brief defines them. `prompt-comparison.md:109–111` promised the discrepancy panel "survives into the assignment proper"; `regulation-shortlist.md:101–103` said "The W2 work is to cut, not to add"; README.md:117–119 says the citywide run is "too large to be the assignment tool". The student diagnosed the risk correctly and then shipped the thing he diagnosed. The engineering leapt forward; the argument retreated.

## The first sixty seconds

0–10 s: a pale street map centred on the 1300 block of E 17th Ave at zoom 16.5 (245), the text "Loading about 62,000 lots…" (153), slider disabled, while 53 MB of GeoJSON downloads (17,197,704 + 36,184,268 bytes, confirmed from the live server). 10–30 s: every lot turns three-quarters red with a blue band along its north edge; dark roofs sit on top (the slider defaults to 75, so every 2015 house is visible); three roofs on the block are amber with amber-outlined lots (1335, 1355, 1383 E 17th: roof shares 50.2 %, 53.9 %, 55.2 %). The panel reads "Impermeable: 75% / Permeable: 25%", then a 22 px amber "13,594 potential roof-coverage exceedances … 21.8% of 62,248 sites", then "39% of the rain soaks into the ground", "717,300 m³ overflows to the storm sewer, about 287 Olympic swimming pools", "A typical (median) 403 m² lot sends about 9,600 L to the sewer", "Past ~40% hardscape the soil is full". 30–60 s: dragging the slider left, red recedes toward the south edge and houses blink out one by one as it passes below their roof share; by 30 % most of the block is empty lots. Clicking 1313 E 17th gives "Lot area: 560.1 m² / Building rule (50% max): mapped roof coverage 44.6% — within. / Impermeable-material rule (75% max): The roof alone is within 75%; other impermeable surfaces are not mapped. / Slider scenario: 75% impermeable (420.1 m²), 25% permeable (140.0 m²). / In a 48 mm storm: 10,500 L soaks in, 13,400 L overflows". "Zoom out to the whole city" shows amber cluster circles with counts across Vancouver.

What they would conclude: this is a stormwater map; paving sends rain to the sewer; a fifth of Vancouver's houses are too big. They would not learn that a paver driveway counts as impermeable, or that a by-law definition exists.

## Findings

**1. MAJOR — The tool contains none of the knowledge it claims to teach.**
Evidence: README.md:10–15 names the definitional gap as "what the tool is for". `grep -i "paver|decking|spaced|gravel|Section 2|definition" map/index.html` returns nothing. No passage text, no page reference, no link to a PDF appears in the page; s. 3.2.2.7 and s. 3.2.2.8 are labels only (173, 178). The Section 2 PDF is not in the repository: sources/README.md:13 still reads "**not yet stored in this repo** … Download before W2 Friday" on the final due date, and `git ls-files sources/` confirms it. The definition exists in the repo only inside a W1 `.jsx` string and a markdown table.
Why: the brief's first capability is "Explain: Ask what the selected provision means and see the relevant source passage" and "A citation should point to a passage that supports the actual claim" (brief pp. 2, 5). Both fail.
Fix: put the two definitions in the panel verbatim with "Section 2, p. 20 / p. 31", commit the PDF, and make the definitions operative (finding 2).

**2. MAJOR — The slider is a parameter sweep, not an operation on the provision.**
Evidence: `gl.uniform1f(loc.u_hard, +slider.value / 100)` (429) sends one value to every lot; the fragment shader colours the southern fraction red regardless of where the building is (333). `min="0" max="75"` (165): the limit is the end of the range, so nothing in the tool can ever exceed s. 3.2.2.8; the only fail state is 16 purple roofs (16 of 62,248 lots have roof share > 0.75, verified). The popup reports the same 75/25 split for every lot, scaled by area (657–658). The only real computation is `waterBalance` (582–590) with P = 48, C = 0.85, D = 75 (222–224), none of which comes from the by-law.
Why: "Change an input and obtain a result produced by an explicit operation" (brief p. 2). A limit teaches by being crossed; this slider cannot cross it. And "Feature count and visual polish alone do not demonstrate successful knowledge translation" (p. 5).
Fix: in the popup, let the visitor assign the non-roof area of the clicked lot among concrete, permeable pavers, spaced-board decking, gravel and lawn; sum with the roof per s. 4.2.2 ("includes site coverage for all buildings", p. 16); test against 75 %. The calc block already exists at `r1-1-coverage-checker.jsx:144–174`. Let the slider reach 100, or remove it.

**3. MAJOR — The 50 % "exceedance" apparatus is the loudest element on the page, and it is the wrong rule on the wrong data against the wrong year.**
Evidence: `13,594` in 22 px amber (691–694; `.risk-count b`, 128) with "potential roof-coverage exceedances". buildings.py caveats 1 and 3: footprints are 2015, "follow the ROOF EDGE, eaves included", and "will usually be a little larger than the by-law's building area". R1-1 replaced the RS schedules on 17 Oct 2023 (HANDOFF.md:28); a house standing in 2015 was permitted under a different schedule, and a zoning by-law regulates development, not existing buildings. Three separate encodings serve this one check: amber roof fill (465), amber lot outline (820–832), amber cluster circles (793–819). The student's own shortlist names s. 3.2.2.8, not 3.2.2.7, as the provision (regulation-shortlist.md:17).
Why: a visitor zooming out sees amber circles in the thousands and concludes a fifth of Vancouver is in breach. "Potential" and "approximate" cannot carry that weight; this is a claim with no corresponding result.
Fix: cut to one grey line of context ("2015 roof share, eaves included") and drop the clusters and the word exceedance; or reframe honestly as a data-quality flag.

**4. MAJOR — Required deliverables are absent from the published package.**
Evidence: `git ls-files` lists 20 files: no skill file, no Rhino or 3D file (`pitch: 0, maxPitch: 0 // flat 2D map`, 305), no screenshot or GIF (zero image files), no test cases, no reflection. GitHub API: 0 issues on the repository. The wrap-up README order (Purpose, How to use with the Pages link "at the top", Source with version and clause, One example as screenshot, Skill and limits) is not followed: the live link is at README.md:91 under "## Live map", there is no example, and the clause numbers are scattered in prose (55–56). Brief p. 2 requires "Visualize: See the relationship represented in Rhino". The README also contradicts the repo: 130–132 says outputs are "neither … committed" while `map/data/*.geojson` (53 MB) are tracked against `.gitignore`'s `map/data/`; 167 promises "A synthetic example will be added for W3"; 117–119 still describes the cut as a future W2 task.
Fix: rewrite the README to the five wrap-up headings; one screenshot of one lot with its numbers and page references; a `skill/` folder; one Rhino lot (the shortlist's own idea, regulation-shortlist.md:65–70, of extruding the shed storm volume would do).

**5. MAJOR — Scope runs against the brief and against the student's own plan.**
Evidence: regulation-shortlist.md:101–103 "stormwater.py is a citywide policy analysis, and the brief asks for something small enough to explain and verify. The W2 work is to cut, not to add." README.md:42 "Precursor, not the assignment tool." The map is that analysis with a slider: 62,248 lots and a 53 MB download before anything is interactive. Brief p. 2: "one primary operation and a manageable set of inputs … use a small example appropriate to your subject."
Why: a uniform percentage over a city can say nothing true about any lot; a one-lot tool can. The README's "test a real lot against it" (README.md:7–8) is not possible: no lot can be given its own inputs.
Fix: open on one lot; make the city a secondary "context" view.

**6. MINOR — The page disclaims itself more than it explains.**
Evidence (panel and popup text, 150–300 and 634–735): "estimate" ×7, "hypothetical" ×5, "scenario" ×5, "not a …" ×5, "approximate" ×3, "potential" ×3, "not mapped" ×3. Three separate sentences say the result is not a compliance finding (180, 697, 729). The provision itself is three lines (p. 12, p. 16).
Why: when a tool must say five times that it is not what it looks like, the image is wrong, not the caption.
Fix: fix the image (findings 2–3) and most hedges become unnecessary.

**7. MINOR — The lot drawing's semantics contradict the by-law and the README.**
Evidence: red grows from the south edge (333; legend 202) irrespective of the roof, so a dark roof can sit on blue "permeable" ground although Section 2 (p. 20) defines the building footprint as impermeable. Houses vanish as the slider drops (`if (u_hard … < v_rs …) discard`, 461); README.md:51–53 explains the "site starts empty" conceit, but the page defaults to 75 (165), so the visitor meets it backwards: lowering paving demolishes houses. "Hardscape" on the stops (168, 170) and water card names a quantity that includes roofs, against "Impermeable" in the readout (160) and "impermeable materials" in the by-law. The "~40% soil full" stop (674–679), an artefact of D = 75 mm, sits on the same scale as "75% bylaw maximum": the flattening of authority sources/README.md:39–41 warns against.
Fix: colour the roof as part of the impermeable share and fill from the house outward; default each lot to its roof share; one vocabulary; move the soil threshold off the by-law scale.

**8. MINOR — Nothing in the tool lets a user check a result against the source.**
Evidence: the popup for 1313 E 17th says "mapped roof coverage 44.6% — within" (654–655) with no clause text, page or link. Brief p. 5: "Can they find the passage supporting an explanation?"
Fix: a "Source" line in panel and popup linking to the PDF page in `sources/`.

## What works

The numbers hold. Recomputing from `lots.geojson`: 62,248 in-scope lots, median 403.0 m², soil-full share 0.398, and at 75 %: 39.1 % held, 717,310 m³ to sewer, 287 pools, 9,612 L for the median lot, 13,594 roofs over 50 %, 16 over 75 %; every figure on the card and in README.md:68–71 matches. The CONFIGURATION blocks with plain-language comments in all three files are the right discipline. The source register distinguishes by-law, engineering guidance and strategy, and records the Wayback retrieval route rather than hiding it. `regulation-shortlist.md` is a genuinely argued document; the 90 cm anomaly in the tree schedule (lines 135–139) is the sharpest observation in the repository. `prompt-comparison.md` already contains the "one example checkable against the source" the brief asks for, a 500 m² lot that flips on 130 m² of pavers; it is simply in the wrong folder. The WebGL work (custom layer, origin-relative precision, 62k lots at interactive rate) is competent, and the live page matches the repository byte for byte.

## The three changes that would matter most

1. **Make the Section 2 classification the operation.** In the clicked lot's popup, material rows for the non-roof area, with "permeable pavers" and "wood decking (spaced / tight)" as choices; the 75 % test per s. 4.2.2 updates as rows change. The W1 B calc block is ready to port. This single change delivers Explain, Compute and Reuse, and makes the README's purpose true.
2. **Demote the hydrology and remove the 50 % apparatus.** The water card becomes one sentence under the lot result; amber clusters, outlines and the 13,594 headline go. The by-law becomes the headline for the first time.
3. **Ship the package the brief and wrap-up describe.** README in the five wrap-up headings with the Pages link at the top; one screenshot of 1313 E 17th (A = 560.1 m², roof 250.1 m², 44.6 %) with references to p. 12, p. 16, p. 20 and p. 31; Section 2 PDF committed; a `skill/` file; one Rhino lot. Without these, the most sophisticated map in the studio is still an incomplete submission.
