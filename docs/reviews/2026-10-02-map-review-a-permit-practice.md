# Outside review: "Impermeable hardscape on R1-1 lots" (ARCH 540, Assignment 1)

Reviewer lens: zoning plan checker / development permit reviewer, BC municipality. Reviewed 2026-10-02 against the live page (byte-identical to `map/index.html` at commit 54ada95), the published repository, the R1-1 District Schedule (June 2026), Section 2 Definitions (June 2026), the course brief v3 and the A1 wrap-up sheet. All lot numbers below were computed from `map/data/lots.geojson` with Python; all by-law quotations are from the PDF text.

## Verdict

The two numbers at the centre of this tool are right and are cited to the right clauses. s.3.2.2.7 reads "Maximum site coverage for all buildings — 50% of the site area" and s.3.2.2.8 "Maximum area of impermeable materials — 75% of the site area" (R1-1, p.12); s.4.2.2 reads "The maximum area of impermeable materials includes site coverage for all buildings" (p.16). The repository's paraphrases of the Section 2 definitions are faithful to the text. The data pipeline is more careful than most professional GIS work I see: parcels are merged into sites on the Section 2 "adjoining parcels" test, footprints are clipped to site lines, suspicious results are flagged, and the hedging language ("not a compliance finding") is present. The water arithmetic reproduces exactly (717,310 m³, 39.1% held, soil full at 39.8%).

The page as published is nevertheless not safe to repeat. It puts the word EXCEEDANCE beside 13,594 real civic addresses on a measurement that its own author documents as biased upward, and most of those flags sit inside that bias. It renders data artefacts as its most severe category. It never shows the definitions the README says are the whole point of the tool, and the package does not contain the document they come from. It opens at the by-law maximum under a title that reads as a survey. And the 48 mm figure is never attributed to anything on the page itself. Each of these is fixable in hours and none needs new data.

## Findings

**1. MAJOR — Most "potential exceedances" lie inside the method's own upward bias.**
Evidence: the by-law measures building area as "the projected area of the outside of the outermost walls of all buildings, including carports, entries, porches and verandahs" (Section 2, "Impermeable Materials", p.20). `buildings.py:48` says the 2015 footprints "follow the ROOF EDGE, eaves included." The page prints "13,594 potential roof-coverage exceedances" and "21.8% of 62,248 sites have mapped roof area above 50%" (`index.html:692-693`), outlines each in amber, and the popup says "POTENTIAL EXCEEDANCE from roof area alone" (`:655`). Of the 13,594 flagged in-scope sites, 5,105 (38%) have a roof share of 50–52%, 9,725 (72%) 50–55%, and 12,023 (88%) 50–58%. A 10 × 14 m house (wall footprint 140 m², perimeter 48 m) with a 0.3 m eave has a roof-edge area of 154.8 m² (+11%); with 0.45 m, 162.4 m² (+16%). A building lawfully at exactly 50.0% wall coverage therefore reads 55–58% in this data. Hand check at the block where the map opens: **1335 E 17th Av**, A = 373.81 m², r = 187.52 m², share 50.16%, margin over 50% = 0.62 m², against roughly 17 m² of eave on a 56 m perimeter. It is flagged, outlined amber, and the popup says POTENTIAL EXCEEDANCE. **1355 E 17th Av** (53.9%) and **1383 E 17th Av** (55.2%) are flagged likewise, all within one eave of the line. The footprints are also from 2015; anything rebuilt since is wrong in either direction.
Why it matters: a homeowner at 1335 E 17th reading this would believe the City has a finding against their house. A journalist would quote "one in five Vancouver houses exceeds the coverage limit." Neither is supported by the data.
Fix: flag only above a stated tolerance (roof share above about 58–60%, documented as "50% plus a typical eave allowance"); show 50–58% as a separate "within measurement tolerance of 50%" class; drop the word EXCEEDANCE in favour of "roof-edge share above 50% (eaves included, 2015)"; never print a civic address beside that phrase without the year in the same line.

**2. MAJOR — The page discards the pipeline's own data-quality flags and publishes artefacts as the most severe category.**
Evidence: `buildings.py` writes `cd` (roof over 90%: "usually means the data is off") and `f` ("shared address, not adjoining — check") into `lots.geojson`; `index.html` never reads either property (no occurrence of `properties.cd` or `properties.f`). Result: **1725 E Broadway**, key R11-063890, a 141.08 m² sliver with r = 141.08 m² (100.0%), cd = 1, f = 1, one of nineteen parcels carrying that address, is drawn purple and its popup reads "The roof alone exceeds the 75% impermeable-material cap." Of the 16 purple sites, 12 are below the 306 m² minimum site area in s.3.2.2.1 (2095 Stainsbury Av 119 m², 4389 Quebec St 163 m², 2502 Wall St 165 m², 525 Kaslo St 190 m², and so on); Section 2 "Site" (p.43) "does not include a strata lot", and sub-minimum parcels under a single building are typically bare-land strata or remnants, that is, not sites at all. The merged-site example the script itself cites, **4010 Victoria Drive** (935 m², roof 680.78 m², 72.8%), is also flagged.
Why it matters: the legend's harshest label is populated almost entirely by things the author's own script says to check.
Fix: render cd = 1 or f = 1 sites grey with "check data — not assessed"; treat parcels below 306 m² (at minimum below 150 m²) the same way.

**3. MAJOR — The definitions the tool exists to teach are absent from the tool, and the document they come from is not in the package.**
Evidence: `README.md:12-15`: "permeable pavers count as impermeable, and wood decking counts as permeable only if the boards are spaced ... That gap between what the words suggest and what the by-law means is what the tool is for." `index.html` contains no occurrence of paver, deck, Section 2, definition, porch, verandah or carport; its only hyperlink is the MapLibre stylesheet; no by-law date or consolidation appears in the visible text. `sources/README.md:13` records Section 2 as "not yet stored in this repo — quoted second-hand via week-01/prompt-b-high-context/. Download before W2 Friday", and the checkout's `sources/` directory has no Section 2 file. The brief requires "Explain: ask what the selected provision means and see the relevant source passage" and says "a citation should point to a passage that supports the actual claim."
A definitional point the README also understates: "Permeable Materials" (p.31) reads "gravel, river rock less than 5 cm in size, wood chips, bark mulch, wood decking with spaced boards and other materials which, in the opinion of the Director of Planning, have fully permeable characteristics when placed or installed on grade with no associated layer of impermeable material, such as plastic sheeting". Section 2 "Deck" (p.11) is "a platform ... generally supported on posts ... [with] a surface height, at any point, greater than 600 mm above grade"; "Patio" (p.30) is at or below 600 mm. A typical raised deck is not "on grade": spaced boards are necessary, not sufficient. "Wood" itself is impermeable (p.20). None of this is on the page.
Fix: add a "What counts" panel quoting both definitions verbatim with "Zoning and Development By-law No. 3575, Section 2, June 2026 consolidation, pp. 20 and 31" and a link; commit the Section 2 PDF; make the panel reachable from the lot popup.

**4. MAJOR — The page opens at the by-law maximum, under a title that reads as a survey, using a term the by-law does not use.**
Evidence: `index.html:165` `value="75"`; `:156` "Impermeable hardscape on R1-1 lots"; readout "Impermeable: 75%"; `:170` "75% hardscape / bylaw maximum"; zoom-18 labels concatenate hypothetical and measured figures as "P 25 / I 75 · Roof 50%". The by-law regulates "impermeable materials", which by s.4.2.2 includes building coverage; "hardscape" in landscape practice means paving excluding buildings. R1-1 has no hardscape maximum.
Why it matters: the first screenshot anyone takes shows every R1-1 lot in Vancouver three-quarters red. The word "hypothetical" is present, in the 12 px grey subtitle.
Fix: open the slider at 0% (or at each site's roof share, which the shader already supports); retitle "Impermeable materials on R1-1 lots — a hypothetical scenario"; replace "hardscape" with "impermeable materials (incl. buildings)" throughout.

**5. MAJOR — The Rain City Strategy is separated from the by-law in the repository but not on the page.**
Evidence: "Rain City" appears once in `index.html`, as a code comment (`:222`). Visible text says "During a design storm" (`:184`) and "The 48 mm storm is environmental context, not a zoning pass/fail test" (`:272`). The "~40% soil full" tick (`:169`) sits on the same slider as "75% bylaw maximum". The water card sits between the two by-law rule boxes and the legend. `stormwater.py` caveats 1–2 and `sources/README.md` state that the 48 mm standard is not a requirement for R1-1, that the earlier 24/48 mm rule left the by-law on 1 January 2024, and that the City's own equation has no runoff coefficient. None of that reaches the page.
Why it matters: "design storm" is a term of art implying a standard. A reader cannot tell whether 48 mm is a by-law figure, a policy figure, or the author's. Two thresholds on one control, one legal and one hydrological, invite the inference that "~40%" means something to the City.
Fix: on the card: "48 mm is the daily design standard of the Rain City Strategy (2019), a Council policy, not a by-law. The R1-1 schedule sets no retention requirement. The 85% runoff and 75 mm soil storage are this page's assumptions." Put the card under a visible "Not the by-law" divider and give the soil-full tick a different colour and the label "hydrology assumption".

**6. MINOR — Rounding contradicts the flag.**
Evidence: the popup prints the roof share to one decimal; 126 flagged lots would print "mapped roof coverage 50.0% — POTENTIAL EXCEEDANCE" (e.g. 3007 Crown St: 172.18 / 344.17 = 50.03%). The zoom-18 label rounds to the integer: 1,399 lots read "Roof 50%" while outlined amber as over 50%.
Fix: adopt the tolerance in finding 1; failing that, flag only when the displayed figure exceeds 50.

**7. MINOR — "Building coverage may not exceed 50% of the site" (`index.html:269`) is true only for s.3.2 uses.**
Evidence: s.3.2 opens "All other uses not regulated by section 3.1 of this schedule are subject to the following regulations" (p.12). s.3.1 (multiple dwelling, pp.8–10, regulations 3.1.2.1–3.1.2.13, read whole) contains no site coverage or impermeable materials regulation. s.2.2.4: "Laneway house is regulated by Section 11 of this by-law and sections 3 and 4 of this schedule do not apply" (p.5). The 2015 footprints include laneway houses and detached garages, all counted. Section 11 is not in the package.
Fix: say "for single detached houses and duplexes under s.3.2"; carry the open laneway-house question from `HANDOFF.md` and `regulation-shortlist.md` into the page notes.

**8. MINOR — "Excludes eaves" is an inference presented as text.**
Evidence: `buildings.py:48-50` "The by-law measures building coverage to the outside of the walls and excludes eaves"; `README.md:79` "The footprints include eaves". The word "eave" appears nowhere in Section 2 or the R1-1 schedule; "site coverage" is not a defined term in Section 2 (it appears only as a diagram label, "Site area coverage %", on the FSR diagram). The reading is reasonable — walls are not eaves — but it rests on the "Impermeable Materials" definition, and projections are governed by Section 10, which is not in the repository.
Fix: cite it as "inferred from Section 2 'Impermeable Materials' (outermost walls); Section 10 governs projections and has not been checked".

**9. MINOR — Scope leaks into the 62,248 "ordinary lots".**
Evidence: 249 in-scope sites have an empty address (104,413 m² in total); two "1 CASSIAR CONNECTOR" merged highway parcels (1,494 and 1,495 m², roof 0) are in scope and in the storm totals; 869 in-scope lots have no roof at all; 2,600 are below the 306 m² minimum site area (1,034 of them flagged).
Fix: mark empty-address and sub-150 m² parcels as "not a lot" (`sc = 2`).

**10. MINOR — Package completeness against the brief.**
Evidence: the published checkout (HEAD 54ada95) has no Rhino geometry or script, no skill file, no test cases, no example screenshot in the README, no annotated source extract, and no links to review issues; `README.md:21` says the map "is the current tool". The brief lists each of these in the final package and the wrap-up sheet asks for "one example ... shown as a screenshot". If these exist locally, they are not in the public repository that classmates will review.

## What works

The by-law numbers and clause references are correct, and the s.4.2.2 relationship (buildings count toward the 75%) is stated correctly on the page ("Roof area is included in the 75% impermeable-material total"). The repository's paraphrases of the Section 2 lists are faithful, including the two genuine surprises, permeable pavers and spaced-board decking. The site-merging logic follows the Section 2 "adjoining parcels" test rather than taking parcels at face value, and its limits are written down. The pipeline flags its own suspicious results and prints them. The water card hedges in the right places ("not a bylaw pass/fail test"), the formula is shown on the page, and the README numbers reproduce from the data to the cubic metre. The code is readable by a non-coder, with every constant explained. The W1 prompt comparison, which catches a superseded 60% figure, is exactly the kind of source discipline the brief asks for.

## The three changes that would matter most

1. **Stop calling 50.2% an exceedance.** Introduce a documented tolerance band (50–58%: "within one eave of the limit"), honour the `cd` and `f` flags on the page, and remove the word EXCEEDANCE from anything printed beside a civic address. This alone turns 13,594 accusations into a defensible map of where roofs are large.
2. **Put the definitions on the page and the document in the repo.** Quote Section 2 pp. 20 and 31 verbatim, with the June 2026 date and a link, and commit the Section 2 PDF. Without this the tool does not do the one thing its README says it is for.
3. **Change the opening state and attribute the 48 mm.** Open at 0%, retitle as a scenario, replace "hardscape" with the by-law's term, and name the Rain City Strategy on the water card as a policy with the page's own assumptions listed beside it.
